Yield and loss

How to measure syrup, cap and preform yield on a bottling line

By Muhammad Ahmad Naeem · 15 November 2026

Most bottling plants know their line efficiency to the decimal point. Far fewer can say how much syrup, how many caps and how many preforms they lose for every thousand bottles packed. I built a material yield model for a PET line in Sydney, and these are the choices that made it work.

Define yield the same way for every material

Yield is what the product should have used divided by what the plant really used. What it should have used comes from the bill of materials multiplied by good production. What it really used is the part people get wrong.

Take actual usage from stock counts, not goods issues

I used opening stock plus receipts minus closing stock. Goods issued to a line are not the same as goods consumed by it. Material is often issued by the full pallet, and in many plants components are posted automatically at the standard quantity when production is confirmed. In that case the system shows a perfect yield every day, and the real loss only appears as a stock adjustment at the next count.

Get the units right before anything else

Syrup is measured in litres but the product is sold in bottles, so syrup yield depends on the mix ratio for each product. Caps and preforms are counted per piece but arrive by the box or the pallet. One wrong conversion will swamp any real loss you are trying to find.

Report at more than one level

I calculate yield per process order, per day, per product and as a weekly and monthly trend. Process order level shows which changeover or which run lost material. The monthly trend smooths out counting errors and shows whether the line is improving.

Expect the daily numbers to be noisy

A stock count taken an hour late moves material from one day to the next. Short runs make it worse. Treat a single bad day as a question and a bad month as a fact.

Know which materials apply to which line

A line that blows its own bottles has a preform yield. A line that buys bottles already blown does not, and its bottle loss needs its own measure. Can lines and carton lines need their own versions again.

Keep the raw data separate from the report

I use one staging workbook that takes the SAP exports and cleans them, and a separate Power BI model that reads only the cleaned tables. When an export format changes, only the staging step needs fixing.

Once yield sits next to efficiency on the same report, material loss stops being a finance surprise at month end and becomes something the line can act on the same week.
