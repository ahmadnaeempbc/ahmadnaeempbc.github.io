How a PET bottling line works, from preform to pallet

By Muhammad Ahmad Naeem · 21 November 2026

A PET bottling line turns a small plastic preform and a tank of syrup into wrapped pallets of drinks, often at tens of thousands of bottles an hour. Here is the line in the order the bottle sees it.

Blow moulding

Preforms are heated in an oven, then stretched with a rod and blown with high pressure air inside a mould to form the bottle. Oven temperatures and blowing pressure decide bottle quality.

Mixing

Treated water is deaerated, blended with syrup at a set ratio, carbonated and cooled. The mixer has to hold that ratio and the carbonation level steady while the filler speeds up and slows down.

Filling and capping

The filler fills each bottle under pressure so the drink keeps its carbonation, and the capper applies the closure straight away. Fill level, cap torque and cap position are checked just after.

Labelling and coding

Labels go on, and a date and batch code is printed so every bottle can be traced.

Packing and palletising

Bottles are collated into packs, shrink wrapped or cased, stacked by a palletiser and stretch wrapped for the warehouse.

The conveyors in between

Conveyors and accumulation tables are easy to ignore, but they decide how well the machines run together. Good accumulation lets one machine stop briefly without stopping the line.

The utilities behind it

Treated water, CO2, high and low pressure compressed air, chilled water, steam and cleaning in place systems. When a utility drifts, the line follows.

Most lost time on these lines does not come from big breakdowns. It comes from changeovers and from short stops: a jammed cap chute, a fallen bottle at a lane divider, a label reel change. That is where I look first.
