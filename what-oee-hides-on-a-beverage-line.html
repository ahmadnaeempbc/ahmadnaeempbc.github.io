What OEE hides on a beverage line

By Muhammad Ahmad Naeem · 21 November 2026

Overall equipment effectiveness multiplies availability, performance and quality into one percentage. It is a useful headline. On a beverage line it also hides most of what you need to know to improve.

Check what counts as planned time

If changeovers and cleaning are excluded as planned stops, OEE can look healthy while the line spends hours a week not making product. Agree the definition once and keep it.

Know which speed you are measuring against

Performance depends on the rated speed, and rated speed changes with pack size. A line measured against a speed it has never reached will always look poor, and one measured against a soft target will always look fine.

Count the short stops

Stops of a minute or two rarely get logged by hand, yet together they are often the largest loss. Line data that captures every stop tells a different story from the shift report.

Find the constraint

The filler usually sets the pace. A stop on the labeller or packer only costs output once the accumulation between them is full, so the same fault can be harmless or expensive depending on where it happens.

Split the losses by cause

Breakdowns, changeovers, minor stops, speed loss and rejects need different fixes. A single percentage cannot tell you which one to work on.

I use OEE to see whether a line is getting better over months. To decide what to fix this week, I use the loss list underneath it, ranked by hours.
