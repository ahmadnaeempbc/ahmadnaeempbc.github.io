Rebuilding a dead nitrogen doser into pulse dosing

By Muhammad Ahmad Naeem · 21 November 2026

Still water has no carbonation to hold a light bottle firm, so a small dose of nitrogen goes in before the cap. It pressurises the bottle, protects its strength in the stack and helps product quality. On our water line at PepsiCo the dosing unit had been effectively dead for years.

What was going wrong

With the unit out of action, dosing had become manual. Nitrogen could keep flowing far longer than a bottle needed. That meant heavy wastage, a dose that changed from bottle to bottle, and constant frustration for the people running the line.

The proposal

I inspected the system, worked out what could be reused, and proposed a retrofit to my manager instead of a new machine. Once it was approved I rebuilt it as precision pulse dosing.

What changed

Dosing went from continuous flow and guesswork to a short pulse for every bottle. A sensor detects the bottle, the PLC fires the pulse, and a solenoid actuated mechanism that I designed and had fabricated delivers it.

Tuning the pulse

The timing was tuned through trials until each bottle size got the dose it needed. The final settings were about 0.20 seconds for the 1.5 litre bottle and about 0.12 seconds for the 0.5 litre bottle.

The result

Nitrogen consumption fell by about 40 percent, worth roughly PKR 20 million a year. Dosing became consistent, and the operators got control back.

The lesson I took from it is to look at what is already bolted to the line before asking for capital. A dead machine is often one sensor, one valve and some logic away from working better than it did when it was new.
