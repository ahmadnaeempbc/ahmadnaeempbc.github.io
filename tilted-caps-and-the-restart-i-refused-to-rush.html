Root cause

Tilted caps, a vibrating capper and the restart I refused to rush

By Muhammad Ahmad Naeem · 15 November 2026

One of the longest shifts of my time at PepsiCo started with tilted caps coming off a PET line. A tilted cap can leak, lose carbonation or fail in a customer's hand, so it is not a defect you run through.

The obvious suspect was not the whole story

The capper was vibrating in a way it should not. It would have been easy to stop there, deal with the vibration and restart. When we kept looking, the vibration had company. Star wheel alignment, the guides and the bottle handling into the capper all had problems at the same time.

A capping problem is often a bottle handling problem

A capper can only apply a cap squarely to a bottle that arrives square, at the right height and at the right moment. Before blaming the capping heads, I now check everything that presents the bottle to them: star wheels, guides, transfer points and timing. What you see is rarely where it started.

I stayed until it was fixed properly

I was on site for more than 24 hours with the maintenance and quality teams while we worked through it.

The pressure to restart is the real test

A stopped line costs money every minute, and there was a production target to hit. Restarting early would have looked good on that shift's numbers. It would also have meant packing product we could not stand behind. I refused to rush it, and I would make the same call again.

Three things I took from that day:

  1. When a fault has an obvious cause, keep looking until you are sure it is the only one.
  2. Agree the restart conditions with quality before the pressure builds, not during it.
  3. One long, proper fix costs less than three short ones.
